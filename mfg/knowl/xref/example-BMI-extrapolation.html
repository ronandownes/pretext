Example 1.157.

  1. Use linear interpolation to estimate the BMI of Miss America 1960.
    🔗
  2. Use linear extrapolation to predict the BMI of Miss America 2001.
    🔗
🔗

Solution.

  1. For 1960, we substitute \(t = \alert{40}\) into the regression equation we found in Example 1.153.
    \begin{equation*} y = 20.68 - 0.04(\alert{40}) = 19.08 \end{equation*}
    We estimate that Miss America 1960 had a BMI of 19.08. (Her BMI was actually 18.79.)
    🔗
  2. For 2001, we substitute \(t = \alert{81}\) into the regression equation.
    \begin{equation*} y = 20.68 - 0.04(\alert{81}) = 17.44 \end{equation*}
    🔗
🔗
Our model predicts that Miss America 2001 had a BMI of 17.44. In fact, her BMI was 20.25. By the late 1990s, public concern over the self-image of young women had led to a reversal of the trend toward ever-thinner role models.
