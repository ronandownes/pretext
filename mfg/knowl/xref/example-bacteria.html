Example 4.3.

A colony of bacteria starts with \(300\) organisms and doubles every week.
  1. Write a formula for the population of the bacteria colony after \(t\) weeks.
    🔗
    🔗
  2. How many bacteria will there be after \(8\) weeks? After \(5\) days?
    🔗
    🔗
🔗

Solution.

  1. The initial value of the population was \(P_0 = 300\text{,}\) and its weekly growth factor is \(b = 2\text{.}\) Thus, a formula for the population after \(t\) weeks is
    \begin{equation*} P(t) = 300 \cdot 2^t \end{equation*}
    🔗
    🔗
  2. After \(8\) weeks, the population will be
    \begin{equation*} P(\alert{8}) = 300 \cdot 2^\alert{8} = 76,800 \text{ bacteria} \end{equation*}
    Because \(5\) days is \(\frac{5}{7}\) of a week, after \(5\) days the population will be
    \begin{equation*} P\left(\alert{\frac{5}{7}}\right)= 300 \cdot 2^{\alert{5/7}} = 492.2 \end{equation*}
    We cannot have a fraction of a bacterium, so we round to the nearest whole number, \(492\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗
