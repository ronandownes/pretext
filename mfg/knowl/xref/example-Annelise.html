Example 1.2.

Annelise is on vacation at a seaside resort. She can rent a bicycle from her hotel for $3 an hour, plus a $5 insurance fee. (A fraction of an hour is charged as the same fraction of $3.)
🔗
  1. Make a table of values showing the cost, \(C\text{,}\) of renting a bike for various lengths of time, \(t\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗
  2. Plot the points on a graph. Draw a curve through the data points.
    🔗
    🔗
  3. Write an equation for \(C\) in terms of \(t\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗
🔗

Solution.

  1. To find the cost, we multiply the time by $3, and add the result to the $5 insurance fee. For example, the cost of a 1-hour bike ride is
    \begin{align*} \text{Cost}\amp=(\$5\text{ insurance fee})+(\$3\text{ per hour})\times(\alert{1}\text{ hour})\\ C\amp=5+3(\alert{1})=8 \end{align*}
    A 1-hour bike ride costs $8. We record the results in a table, as shown here:
    🔗
    Length of rental
    (hours)    		 Cost of rental
    (dollars)    		 \((t,C)\)
    \(1\) \(8\) \(\quad C=5+3(\alert{1})\quad\) \((1,8)\)
    \(2\) \(11\) \(C=5+3(\alert{2})\) \((2,11)\)
    \(3\) \(14\) \(C=5+3(\alert{3})\) \((3,14)\)
    🔗
  2. Each pair of values represents a point on the graph. The first value gives the horizontal coordinate of the point, and the second value gives the vertical coordinate. The points lie on a straight line, as shown in the figure. The line extends infinitely in only one direction, because negative values of \(t\) do not make sense here.
    🔗
    🔗
  3. To write an equation, we let \(C\) represent the cost of the rental, and we use \(t\) for the number of hours:
    🔗
    \begin{align*} \text{Cost}\amp=(\$5\text{ insurance fee})+(\$3\text{ per hour})\times\text{(number of hours)}\\ C\amp=5+3\cdot t \end{align*}
    🔗
    🔗
