Activity 3.4 Obtuse Angles.

🔗
Recall that an obtuse angle has measure between \(90\degree\) and \(180\degree\text{.}\) Use the coordinate definitions of the trig ratios described above to complete the Activity.
🔗
    1. Draw an angle \(\theta\) in standard position with the point \(P(6,4)\) on its terminal side.
      🔗
    2. Find \(r\text{,}\) the distance from the origin to \(P\text{.}\)
      🔗
    3. Calculate \(\sin (\theta),~ \cos (\theta)\text{,}\) and \(\tan (\theta)\text{.}\) Give both exact answers and decimal approximations rounded to four places.
      🔗
    4. Use the inverse cosine key on your calculator to find \(\theta\text{.}\) Use your calculator to verify the values of \(\sin (\theta),~ \cos (\theta)\text{,}\) and \(\tan (\theta)\) that you found in part (3).
      🔗
    🔗
    🔗
    1. Draw another angle \(\phi\) in standard position with the point \(Q(-6,4)\) on its terminal side.
      🔗
    2. Explain why \(\phi\) is the supplement of \(\theta\text{.}\) (Hint: Consider the right triangles formed by drawing vertical lines from \(P\) and \(Q\text{.}\))
      🔗
    3. Can you use the right triangle definitions (using opposite, adjacent and hypotenuse) to compute the sine and cosine of \(\phi\text{?}\) Why or why not?
      🔗
    4. Calculate \(\sin (\phi),~ \cos (\phi)\text{,}\) and \(\tan (\phi)\) using the coordinate definitions. How are the trig values of \(\phi\) related to the trig values of \(\theta\text{?}\)
      🔗
