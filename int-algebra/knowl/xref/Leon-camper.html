Example 1.1.12.

Leon’s camper has a 20-gallon gas tank, and he gets 12 miles to the gallon. (Note that getting 12 miles to the gallon is the same as using \(\frac{1}{12}\) gallon of gas per mile.)
🔗
  1. Write an equation for the amount of gasoline, \(g\text{,}\) left in Leon’s tank after he has driven for \(d\) miles.
    🔗
    🔗
  2. Make a table of values for the equation.
    🔗
    🔗
  3. Graph the equation.
    🔗
    🔗
  4. If Leon has less than 5 gallons of gas left, how many miles has he driven since his last fill-up? Illustrate on the graph.
    🔗
    🔗
🔗

Solution.

  1. We use the form for a linear model,
    \begin{equation*} y=(\text{starting value})+(\text{rate})\times t \end{equation*}
    However, in this problem, instead of variables \(y\) and \(t\text{,}\) we use \(g\) and \(d\text{.}\) Leon’s fuel tank started with 20 gallons, and the amount of gasoline is decreasing at a rate of \(\frac{1}{12}\) gallon for every mile that he drives. Thus,
    \begin{equation*} g=20-\frac{1}{12}d \end{equation*}
    🔗
    🔗
  2. Every time Leon drives 12 miles, he uses another gallon of gasoline. So, to make the calculations easier, we choose values that are divisible by 12.
    🔗
    \(d\) (miles) \(0\) \(48\) \(96\) \(144\)
    \(g\) (gallons) \(20\) \(16\) \(12\) \(8\)
    🔗
  3. We scale the values of \(d\) along the horizontal axis, and the values of \(g\) along the vertical axis. Then we plot the points from the table and connect with a line, as shown in the figure below.
    🔗
    🔗
  4. If Leon has less than 5 gallons of gasoline left, then \(g\lt 5\text{,}\) as shown on the \(g\)-axis. Using our model from part (a), we solve the inequality.
    \begin{align*} 20-\frac{1}{12}d \amp \lt 5 \amp\amp\blert{\text{Subtract } 20 \text{ from both sides.}}\\ -\frac{1}{12}d \amp \lt -15 \amp\amp\blert{\text{Multiply by } {-12} \text{ on both sides.}} \\ d \amp\gt 180\amp\amp \begin{array}{l} \blert{\text{Note that we reversed the direction of the}}\\ \blert{\text{the inequality when we multiplied by }{-12}. } \end{array} \end{align*}
    Leon has driven at least 180 miles. The solution is shown on the graph below.
