Example 5.6.17.

  1. Use a graph of \(y = \abs{3x - 6}\) to solve the equation \(\abs{3x - 6} = 9\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗
  2. Use a graph of \(y = \abs{3x - 6}\) to solve the equation \(\abs{3x - 6} = -2\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗
🔗

Solution.

  1. The graph shows the graphs of \(y = \abs{3x - 6}\) and \(y = 9\text{.}\) We see that there are two points on the graph of \(y = \abs{3x - 6}\) that have \(y = 9\text{,}\) and those points have \(x\)-coordinates \(x = -1\) and \(x = 5\text{.}\) We can verify algebraically that the solutions are \(-1\) and \(5\text{.}\)
    \begin{align*} x = \alert{-1}\text{: }\amp \\ \amp ~~ \abs{3(\alert{-1}) - 6} = \abs{-9} = 9\hphantom{00000}\\ x = \alert{5}\text{: }\amp\\ \amp ~~ \abs{3(\alert{5}) - 6} = \abs{9} = 9 \end{align*}
    🔗

    Aside
    \(\alert{\text{[TK]}}~~\)For more practice with absolute value functions, see Section 5.6.2 of the Toolkit.
    🔗
    🔗
  2. There are no points on the graph of \(y = \abs{3x - 6}\) with \(y = -2\text{,}\) so the equation \(\abs{3x - 6} = -2\) has no solutions.
