Example 8.28.

Divide: \(~\dfrac{a-2}{6a^2} \div \dfrac{4-a^2}{4a^2-2a}\)
🔗

Solution.

First, we change the operation to multiplication by taking the reciprocal of the divisor.
\begin{gather*} \dfrac{a-2}{6a^2} \cdot \dfrac{4a^2-2a}{4-a^2} \end{gather*}
Now, we follow the rules for multiplication. We begin by factoring each numerator and denominator. Once we have finished factoring, we can look for any common factors to cancel.
\begin{align*} \dfrac{a-2}{6a^2} \cdot \dfrac{4a^2-2a}{4-a^2} \amp = \dfrac{a-2}{3 \cdot 2 \cdot a \cdot a} \cdot \dfrac{2a(2a-1)}{(2-a)(2+a)}\\ \amp =\dfrac{-1\cancel{\blert{(2-a)}}}{3 \cdot \cancel{\blert{2}} \cdot \cancel{\blert{a}} \cdot a} \cdot \dfrac{\cancel{\blert{2}} \cancel{\blert{a}}(2a-1)}{\cancel{\blert{(2-a)}}(2+a)}\\ \amp \qquad\blert{\text{Multiply remaining factors.}}\\ \amp = \dfrac{-1(2a-1)}{3a(2+a)} = \dfrac{1-2a}{3a(2+a)} \end{align*}
