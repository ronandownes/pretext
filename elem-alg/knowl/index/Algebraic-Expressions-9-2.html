Activity 1.35 Writing Algebraic Expressions.

To write an algebraic expression.

  1. Identify the unknown quantity and write a short phrase to describe it.
    🔗
  2. Choose a variable to represent the unknown quantity.
    🔗
  3. Use mathematical symbols to represent the relationship described.
    🔗
🔗
🔗
Study the Example below, then write expressions in the Exercise.
🔗
EXAMPLE: Write an algebraic expression for each quantity.
  1. 3 feet more than the length of the rug
    🔗
  2. Twice the age of the building
    🔗
🔗
Solution:
  1. The length of the rug is unknown.
    \begin{gather*} \blert{\text{Length of the rug:}~~~l} \end{gather*}
    The words "more than" indicate addition:
    \begin{gather*} \blert{l+3} \end{gather*}
    🔗
    🔗
  2. The age of the building is unknown.
    \begin{gather*} \blert{\text{Age of the building:}~~~a} \end{gather*}
    "Twice" means two times:
    \begin{gather*} \blert{2a} \end{gather*}
    🔗
    🔗

The next example involves subtraction and division.
🔗
EXAMPLE: Write an algebraic expression for each quantity.
  1. 5 square feet less than the area of the circle
    🔗
  2. The ratio of your quiz score to 20
    🔗
🔗
Solution:
  1. The area of the circle is unknown
    \begin{gather*} \blert{\text{Area of circle:}~~~a} \end{gather*}
    The words "less than" indicate subtraction:
    \begin{gather*} \blert{A-5} \end{gather*}
    🔗
    🔗
  2. Your quiz score is unknown.
    \begin{gather*} \blert{\text{Quiz score:}~~~s} \end{gather*}
    "Twice" means two times:
    \begin{gather*} \blert{\dfrac{s}{20}} \end{gather*}
    🔗
    🔗
